About this page

Where the numbers come from

Everything below is pulled from the pixelmesh detection pipeline's own debug capture of the MaccTech meetup in Macclesfield, 22 July 2026: per-phone decode timings, confidence scores, and the controller's annotated video of the run. No estimates; this is the system's own paper trail.

The room

The first perfect detection

Every phone still connected at the end of the run was found. That is a first: at every previous show there were stragglers. The auto-stop ("all clients found") had never fired in front of an audience; tonight it fired twice.

The honest denominator: 21 phones held blink IDs mid-run. Two dropped their connections before the end, almost certainly locked or backgrounded phones, since both were found first in the follow-up run, at 13.6s and 13.9s, right at the warmup floor.

The fix, proven

One log line, one recovered phone

At the Birmingham show, a phone that decoded perfectly was silently discarded for the whole run because it sat within 60px of an already-found neighbour: the detector's phantom-suppression radius treating a real phone as a duplicate. The fix shipped the following week: IDs that belong to connected phones are exempt from that dedup.

Tonight it earned its first live save. The log reads kept valid ID=20 conf=0.93 inside cluster of ID=17: the Birmingham failure mode, caught and kept. Without it, tonight would have been 18 of 19. Alongside it, phantom rejection kept working: a spurious decode of ID 64 was rejected seven times and never placed.

Lock screens
The two mid-run drops point at screens locking, not detection. The wake lock asks; iOS does not always oblige. The real open problem at bigger scale.
One crash
The GUI wedged on a projector unplug (a GLFW display-topology limitation, not detection). Full recovery in ~90s including a 20/20 re-detection. Runbook updated: displays connect before start.
